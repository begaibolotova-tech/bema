# Facts and numbers on screen
- Everest 8 849 m (8 848.86 m, joint China–Nepal survey, 2020). Jengish Chokusu 7 439 m, Khan Tengri 7 010 m (Kyrgyzstan's
  highest peaks; standard figures). Burj Khalifa 828 m. 8 849 / 828 ≈ 10.7 → «10 эсе бийик».
- «аба 2,5 эсе суюк»: ISA air density at 8 849 m ≈ 0.39 of sea level (1.225 → ~0.48 kg/m³), i.e. ~2.6× thinner (fall.py).
- «−20…−35 °C»: typical summit temperatures (summer ≈ −20 °C, winter ≈ −35 °C; e.g. Matthews et al., 2020, Everest
  summit weather stations, National Geographic "Perpetual Planet" expedition).
- «шамал 200 км/саатка чейин»: jet-stream winds at the summit regularly exceed 160–200 km/h in winter (same source).
- The fall (chart, readouts, «15 секундда ~290 км/саат», «~49 секунд»): a physics model, not a measurement —
  fall.py: 80 kg, belly-down, drag tuned to 200 km/h terminal at sea level, ISA density; vertical fall from
  8 849 m to base-camp altitude 5 364 m. Result: 49.2 s; ~289 km/h at 15 s; peak ~296 km/h.
- «тоо тик дубал эмес»: the profile drawn is a schematic (labelled СХЕМА), not survey data.
- Valery Rozov, 5 Oct 2013: wingsuit BASE jump from 7 220 m on Everest's north side (Changtse), landed safely
  by parachute on the Rongbuk glacier (widely reported, e.g. Red Bull, BBC).
- «Парашютсуз — эч ким тирүү калбайт»: a vertical fall of ~3.5 km at ~280 km/h is not survivable; stated as the
  film's conclusion from the model.
